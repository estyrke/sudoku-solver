# Queens hints are generated from two general primitives, not a catalog of named patterns

Human Queens strategy guides describe elimination techniques as named shapes: two-in-a-line, three-in-a-line, L-shapes, T-shapes, and region/line confinements generalized to pairs and triplets (the Queens analogue of Sudoku's X-Wing/Swordfish). We chose not to encode these as a shape catalog. Instead the engine has two general primitives: **confined-set adjacency-shadow elimination** (for any candidate set S a unit is confined to, the cells adjacent to every cell in S, intersected across S, can be eliminated — this alone reproduces the 2-line, 3-line, L and T patterns as special cases with no shape matching) and **region↔line confinement** generalized to unbounded K via ascending brute-force subset search (feasible because real Queens boards are small, roughly 6x6-11x11). Both families feed one priority queue ordered by the size of S/K, smallest first, rather than a fixed technique list or two separate passes.

The trade-off: a hint's explanation has to describe the geometry in words ("these N cells are the only candidates for {unit}, so {shadow cells} can't hold a Queen") rather than naming a pattern from a strategy guide.
